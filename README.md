# The Omega System

A small phone DAW: 16-pad drum rack with MPC-style real-time recording, sample chopping, instrument tracks with an in-key grid, warped audio tracks from a USB-C interface, scenes and song mode, MIDI clock sync with an MPC, automation, effects (EQ, filter, compressor, saturator, redux, chorus, auto pan, sidechain duck, reverb, delay), SP-1200 12-bit mode, drum machine kits (TR-909, TR-505, LinnDrum LM-2, Oberheim DMX, Boss DR-110, Simmons SDS-V), a master EQ with EQ matching, a talking-machine text-to-speech for the Star Screamer 350 vocoder, the Wheel of Steel turntable with touch and MIDI scratching, an FX rack (Lexicon PrimeTime, Deltalab Effectron, Eventide H910, MXR 113, Lexicon 224 / AMS RMX16), a Prophet-5 model, a TB-303 model, a Moog Taurus model, a Solina String Ensemble model, an SVC-350 vocoder model, stem separation and WAV export.

## Put it online with GitHub Pages
1. Create a GitHub repo and upload these files to it (or push them).
2. In the repo, open Settings, then Pages. Under "Branch", pick `main` and `/ (root)`, then Save.
3. After a minute, open `https://<your-username>.github.io/<repo>/` in Chrome on your phone.
4. In Chrome's menu, tap "Add to Home screen".

## Jam-On Rig (`/jamon/`)
A separate app with the gear from Newcleus's *Jam On Revenge*: TR-808, Oberheim DMX, TB-303, Sequential Pro-One, SH-101, Juno-60, Roland RS-09, a Fender Precision bass (physical model) and an Electro-Harmonix vocoder (EH-0300 model) driven by text to speech or a live mic. Open `https://<your-username>.github.io/<repo>/jamon/` in Chrome and Add to Home screen.
- Seven arrangements in the style of the album's tracks (original patterns, not transcriptions), presets per machine, arpeggiators, a step grid / piano roll per machine.
- MIDI in (USB or Bluetooth keyboards, by channel or to the machine on screen), MIDI file import and export, WAV export and live recording.
- Mix FX: Lexicon PrimeTime, Deltalab Effectron, Eventide H910, Lexicon 224 / AMS RMX16 and a gated room as sends; MXR Phase 90, EHX Small Stone, EHX Electric Mistress, A/DA Flanger and UREI 1176 as inserts; a cymbal bus; dub throws and drops.
- Full MIDI: notes, velocity, sustain, pitch bend, mod wheel, CC7 / CC10, program change, all notes off, MIDI Learn for every control, clock in (tempo, start, stop, continue, song position), clock and notes out.
- Pictures for the About screen go in `jamon/art/` (cover.jpg, band.jpg).
- Band centres for the vocoder are the published EH-0300 list; 808 voice frequencies follow published circuit analyses (Werner, Abel & Smith, DAFx-14, and others).

## Omega Twelve (`/twelve/`): 12-bit sampler emulator
A vintage sampler in your phone or your DAW. Import any audio or video file, pick part of it, and run it through the E-mu SP-1200 or SP-12 simulated at circuit level (the service-manual circuits for the anti-alias filter, outputs 3-6, the SSM2044 and the network that sweeps it, plus the 8-channel DAC timing and the 7-bit pitch counter), checked against recordings of a real SP-12 (`twelve/VALIDATION.md`), or 16 other samplers (MPC60, MPC3000, S612, S950, S1000, RZ-1, SK-1, Emax, EPS, EPS 16+, Mirage, DSS-1, S-550, SU700, TX16W, Clean) with 16 filter models, a driven preamp, alias in semitones on any machine, filter on/off, input/output link, bypass and VU meters.
- Full MIDI (notes, velocity, pitch bend, program change, MIDI learn for every control, clock), and a live TV screen (Cast, or a TV window).
- 540 presets in 27 families, plus a random settings generator and your own presets.
- Tuning (semitones, cents, A4 reference, transpose to a key), lo-fi (vinyl crackle, tape hiss, wow, flutter, tape saturation, vinyl age, mains hum).
- Tempo and key detection, warp to a BPM (stretch keeping pitch, or speed it like the hardware), timestretch: smooth, or cyclic S950 / S1000 style in Classic (stretched, synced to the bar in the plugin) or Warp (length kept, cycles pitched up), with grain down to half a millisecond; the plugin does it live on its input too.
- Select, zoom, truncate, normalize, reverse, undo, resample (bounce what you hear back in, no export needed), 16 multi-pitch pads and MIDI, STOP ALL.
- Master out: EQ matching to a reference track (or a typical mix), 5-band parametric EQ, compressor, gain, look-ahead limiter, Auto master to a loudness target.
- Export WAV (16/24/32-bit float), AIFF, FLAC, MP3, and Opus/OGG/M4A via the device codec.
- Android app: the `twelve-latest` release (`omega-twelve.apk`, built by `.github/workflows/twelve-apk.yml`). Web: `https://<your-username>.github.io/<repo>/twelve/`.
- VST3 (and AU on Mac, plus a standalone app): Omega Twelve (effect: live input through the sampler, and samples from MIDI) and Omega Twelve Instrument, in the `vst3-latest` release. The plugin's engine (`plugins/twelve/Twelve.h`) is a line-for-line port of `twelve/engine.js`; `twelve/tools/compare.mjs` checks they render the same samples.

## Omega SID 64 (`plugins/sid/`, `android-sid/`): the C64's SID chip as a synth
A circuit-level C++ model of the MOS 6581 and 8580, played from a C64: the controls are a 40 x 25 C64 screen in a 1701-style monitor, above a breadbin keyboard with brown keys and the f1-f7 function keys.
- The chip: 24-bit oscillators with triangle, saw, pulse, noise and the combined waveforms; hard sync and ring mod; the envelope's rate counter (and the optional ADSR bug); the uneven R-2R DACs of the 6581; the filter's two-integrator loop with the 6581's signal-dependent FET resistors and its cutoff curve (with chip-to-chip variation), or the 8580's linear one; voice 3 off; the C64's output filters. PAL or NTSC clock.
- The synth: poly over up to 8 SIDs, mono / legato with glide, or one chip with a note per oscillator like a C64; modulation at the frame rate (50 / 60 Hz, or faster) the way C64 music players do it: arpeggios and chords, vibrato, pulse-width sweeps, a filter envelope, attack programs (per-frame waveform / pitch tables for drums, plucks and zaps); echo.
- Sampling into the C64: load a file, record (the plugin's audio input or the standalone / phone microphone) or pick one of 12 built-in sounds, and it's brought down to what a C64 holds: 3.9 to 22 kHz (the NMI timer's whole-cycle rates), 4 or 8 bits, 64K of RAM. The chip model plays it the way C64 code does, by register writes at the timer rate: $D418 4-bit (the volume register; audible through the 6581's mixer offset, nearly silent on an 8580 unless boosted), Mahoney 8-bit (a $D418 table measured on the model, voices held at DC), PWM 8-bit (oscillator 1 reset each sample with the sample as its pulse width; oscillators 2 and 3 stay free), or a Digimax 8-bit DAC. Notes repitch it by changing the timer rate; start, end, loop, one-shot / gated. 17 DIGI presets. The sample is saved with the project.
- The reverb is the Omega REV7 (the Yamaha REV7 model in `plugins/dsp/Rev7.h`, from its service manual): all 30 programs on the REV page, each type showing its own controls as on the unit, the input level, vintage converters, phase and 3-band EQ, and a browser for its 144 presets (factory, artists, library). It runs on the reverb send; SID presets can call one up (`r7p=`).
- An effects mixer (FX page): OSC 1-3 and the filter each send to the echo and the REV7; the returns have faders, mutes and meters, and the echo can feed the reverb.
- A full editing grid (GRID page): patterns are notes with a start, a length, a pitch and a velocity, so a step can hold a chord. Draw by dragging right, drag a note to move it, drag its last cell to resize it, tap it to delete it; a velocity lane and a slide lane; views of steps 1-16, 17-32 or all 32; transpose, shift, copy / paste, double, undo, a default length and velocity. The tracker (SEQ page) edits the same notes. Old saved patterns still load.
- Full MIDI (MIDI page): channel (omni or 1-16), velocity curves, channel and poly aftertouch to cutoff and vibrato, RPN bend range, bank select + program change across all presets, MIDI learn for every control and a default controller map (shown and editable), MIDI clock in (start / stop / continue / song position) driving the sequencer, the sequencer's notes and clock out (the plugin sends them to the host; the phone to its USB / Bluetooth devices), and patterns to and from standard MIDI files.
- A FILE page (DSK): export the pattern (rendered offline for a number of loops plus its tail), the song, or a recording of the output as WAV (16 or 24-bit) or MP3 (128 / 192 / 320 kbps, encoded with shine, `plugins/third_party/shine`, LGPL), with normalize; import and export MIDI files; buttons to the audio mixer (MIX), the effects mixer (FX), the song browser (SNG) and the instrument mixer (SMX).
- Songs (SNG and SMX pages): set a folder of MIDI files once (the plugin and the phone remember it; folders inside it are listed too) and its songs show in a browser; tap one and every instrument in it (a track and channel, up to 8) becomes a part with its own SID synth, its sound picked from the file's General MIDI program, with channel 10 played by a kit of SID drums. Play, stop, rewind, loop and seek; tempo changes, bend and sustain follow the file. The instrument mixer (SMX) has a strip per part: fader, LED meter, pan, mute, solo, and sends to the echo and the REV7, which every part shares with the keyboard (their returns are on the MIX and FX pages). Give a part any preset (< >, or the SOUNDS list), or edit its sound on the keys (EDIT ON KEYS, then KEYS>PART). The song and its mix are saved with the project, and the FILE page exports it as WAV or MP3.
- A mixer page: OSC 1-3 and the filter output each with a fader, LED meter, pan, mute and solo; the echo and REV7 returns and the main output with their meters; the scope underneath. Every mixer control is a plugin parameter (automation).
- A 32-step sequencer (tracker style) with 16 patterns, accent, slide, swing and 41 pattern presets; it follows the DAW's transport, and held keys transpose it.
- 406 presets in 17 groups (bass, lead, pad, keys, pluck, arp, chord, brass, drums, FX, sync, ring, classic, digi, REV7, 8580, 6581), a random patch, MIDI (bend, mod wheel, sustain, CC 74, program change) and a QWERTY keyboard on desktop.
- Android: the `omega-sid` release (`omega-sid-64.apk`, native, low-latency AAudio, USB / Bluetooth MIDI). It turns both ways: upright, the monitor spans the top with big page buttons and the edit panel under it; sideways, they sit either side of it. The padlock on the monitor frame holds it the way it's turned (remembered). Desktop: VST3 (and AU on Mac) plus a standalone app in the same release, built by `.github/workflows/sid-release.yml`.
- Tests: `sid_test` (tuning, filter, mixer, every sampling method on both chips, every preset, sequencer and host sync, songs and their mixer, state).

## Using a USB-C interface
Plug it in, open the Audio tab and tap Enable input. When Chrome asks, allow the microphone. Pick the interface under Setup, then Device if it isn't the default.
