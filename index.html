Getting started

The Snake Device is a small studio for your phone: an MPC-style drum rack, synths, audio tracks you record from a USB-C interface, a mixer with effects, scenes for song parts, and export to WAV, MP3, stems and MIDI.

Install it like an app

  1. Open the app in Chrome.
  2. Tap Chrome's ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen.
  3. Open it from the home screen icon. It runs full-screen and works offline after the first visit (MP3 export needs internet the first time).

Your first beat in a minute

  1. On Drums, tap pads to hear the built-in kit.
  2. Tap Overdub (or the round record button at the top). You get a one-bar count-in, then the loop plays.
  3. Play pads in time. Each pass layers on top. Tap ■ to stop.
  4. Made a mistake? Undo removes the last pass.

Everything saves automatically on this phone. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Transport, tempo and the record button
  • ▶ / ■ starts and stops. The space bar does the same on a keyboard.
  • ● Record depends on the tab: on Audio it records the armed audio track; everywhere else it toggles drum and synth Overdub.
  • Position shows bar.beat.sixteenth. During a count-in it shows “In 1… In 4”.
  • BPM: type a value, use − / +, or tap Tap three or more times in tempo. Range 40–240.
  • Loop: 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars for the current scene.
  • Click turns the metronome on during playback. The count-in always clicks.
  • Count-in can be turned off in Setup → Recording.

When the clock is set to Follow MPC (Setup → MIDI), the tempo controls lock and play/stop come from your MPC.

Drums: real-time recording
  • Pads are laid out like an MPC: A01 bottom-left, A16 top-right. A dot means the pad has hits in this loop.
  • Overdub: play pads while the loop runs and they are recorded. Each pass layers on top.
  • Timing (timing correct): Off keeps hits exactly as played; 1/8, 1/16, 1/32 and triplets snap hits as you play. Apply snaps hits you already recorded.
  • Swing delays every second 1/16 (0–60%). It applies to hits that sit on the 1/16 grid.
  • Note repeat: turn it on, then hold a pad. It retriggers at the Repeat rate in time with the loop; with Overdub on, the repeats are recorded.
  • Erase: turn it on, then hold a pad while the loop plays. Its hits disappear as the playhead passes them.
  • Velocity: Full level plays every hit at full volume. By touch height makes hits louder the higher you strike the pad. Phones can't sense pressure.
  • Timeline: shows every pad's hits across the loop. Tap a hit to delete it, or tap a row to select that pad.
  • Undo steps back through recording passes and edits. Double loop doubles the loop length and copies the hits so you can add variations. Clear all (tap twice) empties the scene's drum hits.

If recorded hits land a little late, raise Setup → Recording → Pad timing. It compensates for touchscreen delay.

Pads and samples
  • Load file puts a WAV, AIFF, MP3, FLAC or OGG file on the selected pad. The file name becomes the pad name.
  • Sample input records from your USB-C interface onto the pad: tap to start, tap again to stop. Silence at the start is trimmed and the level is normalized.
  • Built-in puts the current kit's sound back on the pad.
  • Chop opens the selected pad's sample in the Chop tab.
  • Level, Tune (±24 semitones), Pan and Decay (shortens the sound) work per pad.
  • Mute grp: pads in the same group cut each other off, like closed and open hi-hats.
  • Clear hits (tap twice) removes only the selected pad's hits.
Drum machine kits

Pick a machine from the Kit menu above the pads. All 16 pads change to that machine's sounds; your recorded hits stay. If some pads hold your own samples, you're asked whether to replace them too.

  • Roland TR-909: analog kick (the pitch sweep falls to a fixed ~52 Hz, with an attack click), two-oscillator snare with the snappy noise that holds about 24 ms, analog toms, rim and clap. The hats, ride and crash run through a 6-bit, ~30 kHz sample stage and then an analog decay, like the real machine. Extra pads: long kick, accent kick, snappy and tight snares, accent clap.
  • Roland TR-505: the 16 sounds of the 505, played through an 8-bit, 25 kHz linear sample stage. Includes congas, timbale and two cowbells.
  • LinnDrum LM-2: 8-bit companded (AM6070-style) samples at about 30 kHz. The kick, toms and congas go through the LinnDrum's swept 4-pole filter, which opens on each hit and closes over about 100 ms. Every voice has the 15.9 kHz output filter, and the hats have the 5.6 ms hold before they decay.
  • Oberheim DMX: 8-bit companded samples. Kick, toms, clap and shaker run on the "low" voice cards (6-pole filter at 10 kHz); snare, hats and cymbals run on the "high" cards (16 kHz). Includes the accent hat.
  • Boss DR-110: all analog. The hats and cymbal use the DR-110's metal source: four square waves at 305, 444, 794 and 1136 Hz plus noise, through its 3.45 kHz and 7.12 kHz band-pass filters. The clap is noise through a 910 Hz filter with the 700 ms tail. Accent pads drive the shared VCA into its slight distortion.
  • Simmons SDS-V: each voice is a triangle oscillator with a falling pitch bend that lasts as long as the decay, plus filtered noise and a click. You get five toms from high to floor, a big bend tom, a bend-up tom and a noisy tom.

The sample-based machines (909 cymbals, TR-505, LinnDrum, DMX) don't contain the original factory recordings. Their sounds are modeled studio drums, put through each machine's own converter, bit depth, clock rate and filters, which is where most of each machine's character comes from. Pad Tune, Decay and SP-1200 mode all work on top of any kit.

SP-1200 mode

Turn on SP-1200 mode in the pad card and the whole drum rack runs through a model of the E-mu SP-1200's audio path:

  • Samples are converted like the original: a 7th-order anti-alias filter, sample-and-hold at 26,041.67 Hz (20 MHz ÷ 768) and a truncating 12-bit converter, up to 65,536 samples (about 2.5 s) per sound.
  • Pitch uses drop-sample playback with E-mu's semitone table and a 7-bit fraction counter, including its slight detuning. Range is −16 to +15 semitones, and +12 and up all play at double speed, like the hardware.
  • An 8-bit level DAC sets each hit's volume, and each of the 8 output channels is mono: a new hit on a channel cuts the one before.
  • SP output (per pad): 1–2 go through an SSM2044-style 4-pole transistor-ladder filter that opens on each hit and closes over about a quarter second; 3–6 each have their own fixed low-pass (3 is darkest, 6 is brightest); 7–8 are unfiltered.
  • Dyn cutoff and Dyn reso set where the outputs 1–2 filter settles and how much it resonates, like the sliders on the Rossum reissue.

Tips: put kicks and bass sounds on 1–2 for that filtered thump, snares on 3–4, hats on 7–8. Tune samples down and play them back for the classic gritty sound.

Any track can also get the 12-bit, 26 kHz grit without the rest: Mixer → FX → 12-bit 26k.

The filter data comes from libmd12 (see Credits), which was checked against circuit simulation and a real SP-12. Above about 12 kHz this app's filters cut a few dB more than the analog originals.

Chop: slice samples across pads
  1. Get a sample: Load file, Record (from the input), From pad (the selected pad), or From track (any audio track's clip, including stems).
  2. Slice it: Equal cuts 2–16 even slices; Transients cuts at hits, use Sensitivity for more or fewer; Manual lets you Split the selected slice or Remove its marker.
  3. Adjust: drag a marker by its triangle, or nudge the selected slice's start by ±1 or ±10 ms. Tap a slice or its number to hear it. Slice points snap to zero crossings so they don't click.
  4. Send to pads: pick the first pad; slices fill the following pads (up to 16). This replaces those pads' samples.

Make a pattern writes hits that play the slices back in their original timing, so a chopped break plays as the full loop. Set how many bars the sample is and tap Set BPM to match the tempo to it.

Audio tracks and recording

Set up your interface

  1. Plug your USB-C interface into the phone.
  2. Tap Enable input (Audio tab or Setup) and allow the microphone when Chrome asks.
  3. If it doesn't pick the interface, choose it under Setup → Audio input → Device.
  4. Choose Channels: input 1, input 2, or both as stereo.

Play through the MPC (or any USB device)

With the MPC plugged in by USB-C, pick it under Setup → Audio input → Sound out. If it isn't listed, tap Enable input first so the phone lists your USB devices. Android usually switches to a USB audio device by itself; if the sound stays on the phone, swipe down from the top of the screen, tap the media output button and pick the MPC. The MPC's speaker or headphone output then plays everything in the app.

Record

  • Arm the track to record (only one at a time) and choose its Record length (1–8 bars).
  • Press ●. From stop you get a one-bar count-in; while playing, recording starts at the next loop.
  • Monitor plays the input through the armed track's effects. Use headphones to avoid feedback.
  • Recordings are shifted automatically to allow for the phone's audio delay (latency). If takes land late, turn Setup → Recording → Auto off and raise Offset; if they land early, lower it. Record claps along with the click to check.

Import, warp and clips

  • Import loads an audio file into the track and guesses its bar length from the tempo. Fix it with Clip if the guess is wrong.
  • Clips remember their tempo and follow the project BPM with Warp: Complex stretches without changing pitch (best for vocals and instruments); Beats retriggers each 1/16 (best for drum loops); Re-Pitch speeds up and slows down like a record; Off plays as is.
  • Stems splits the clip into four tracks (see Stem separation).
  • Up to 10 audio tracks. Clips belong to the current scene.
Stem separation

Tap Stems on an audio track with a clip. It splits the clip into Drums, Bass, Vocals and Other as four new tracks that stay in time with the song, and mutes the original. The four stems add up exactly to the original, so nothing is lost.

How it works: the sound is analysed in short slices; steady tones are separated from hits by median filtering, the low steady part becomes Bass, and the steady part in the middle of the stereo image (150 Hz–7 kHz) becomes Vocals.

  • Works best on full stereo mixes with the vocal in the centre. On a mono file, everything steady in the vocal range ends up in Vocals.
  • Expect rougher results than AI tools such as Ableton 12.3's stem separation, which use large neural networks that don't fit in a phone browser.
  • Clips up to 2 minutes. It runs in the background so the app stays responsive.
  • Load the Drums stem into Chop (From track) to chop a break out of a song.
Instrument tracks and the note grid
  • Up to 4 instrument tracks. Tap + at the top of the Synth tab to add one; new tracks start as a Prophet-5.
  • Each track can be a Prophet-5, Solina strings, TB-303 or Moog Taurus: pick it from the menu next to the track name. Each has its own section in this help.
  • The grid only plays notes in the chosen key and scale. Each row is a fourth higher than the one below, like Ableton Push. Orange pads are the root note. Use Oct − / + to move up and down. Choose Chromatic for every note.
  • Keyboard swaps the grid for a piano keyboard of 1, 1½ or 2 octaves starting at C. It plays every note; keys outside the key are shaded and the root notes have an orange dot. Play chords with several fingers, or slide along the keys to play each one in turn. Lower on a key plays louder, unless Full level is on. Oct − / + shifts it.
  • Overdub records what you play, with the same Timing (timing correct) as the drums.
  • Piano roll shows the notes in this scene. Tap a note to delete it, or tap an empty spot to hear that pitch. Undo and Clear work as on the drums.
  • A MIDI keyboard or your MPC can play a track: set Setup → MIDI → Notes play to it.
Solina strings

Any synth track can be a Solina String Ensemble: on the Synth tab, change Synth to Solina strings. It is modeled from the original Solina schematics.

  • Tone: all notes come from one divide-down tone generator, so octaves are phase-locked and each note has the tiny detune of a real top-octave generator chip. Each square wave is shaped by the original capacitor-diode circuit into the slightly hollow Solina sawtooth.
  • Viola plays the 8' rank (as written), Violin the 4' rank (an octave up). Use both for the classic sound.
  • Trumpet and Horn send the tone through the formant circuit for brassy colours.
  • Cello (8') and Contrabass (16') are the mono bass section: the lowest note you hold below C3 plays it, through its own low-pass.
  • Crescendo is one shared swell: the sound fades in when you start playing from silence, and legato notes join without restarting it, like the original's single VCA.
  • Sustain lets each key ring on after release; the slider sets how long (the value shown is the circuit's time constant).
  • Ensemble is the famous three-line chorus: three delay lines around 2.8 ms, swept 120° apart by the slow chorus oscillator (about 0.75 Hz) plus the faster tremolo (about 6.3 Hz). Turn it off to hear the raw organ-like tone. Wide spreads the three lines left, centre and right instead of the original mono.
  • Chorus Hz and Tremolo Hz are the trimmers inside the original; Ens depth and Tremolo set how strong each sweep is.

Try it through the Vocoder for the late-70s string-vocoder sound.

Prophet-5

Any synth track can be a Prophet-5: on the Synth tab, change Synth to Prophet-5. It is modeled on the Rev 3 instrument from Sequential's service and owner's manuals, with five voices.

  • Oscillator A: saw and/or pulse (width 1–99%), and Sync to Oscillator B. Frequency steps in semitones over four octaves.
  • Oscillator B: saw, triangle and/or pulse, Fine (up to a semitone up), Lo freq turns it into an LFO (0.4–30 Hz), and Kbd off makes it a drone.
  • Mixer: levels of A, B and white noise into the filter. Turn them up for a hotter, more saturated filter.
  • Filter: the CEM3320 four-pole low-pass. Resonance self-oscillates above about 7. Keyboard makes the cutoff follow the notes, so a self-oscillating filter can be played in tune.
  • Envelopes: filter and amplifier ADSR, about 1 ms to 30 s; the knob is exponential (5 is about half a second). Release off gives a short click-free release.
  • Poly-Mod: the filter envelope and Oscillator B modulate Oscillator A's pitch, pulse width and/or the filter, separately in every voice. Oscillator B into Freq A gives clangy FM bells; the filter envelope into Freq A with Sync on gives the classic sync sweep.
  • LFO and Wheel-Mod: the LFO (0.04–20 Hz, saw/triangle/square) and pink noise, mixed by Source mix, reach the destinations you switch on, with depth set by the Mod wheel. A MIDI keyboard's mod wheel and pitch bend work when Setup → MIDI → Notes play is set to this track.
  • Unison stacks all five voices on the lowest note held, with Glide. Vintage sets how much the five voices differ and drift, like real component tolerances; 0 is perfectly matched.

Like the original, most knobs move in 128 steps, so cutoff and pitch change in semitone steps. Programs give you starting points: Brass, Poly strings, Sync lead, Unison bass, Poly-mod bell, Pad, Pluck and Resonant sweep.

TB-303

Any synth track can be a TB-303 bass line: on the Synth tab, change Synth to TB-303. It follows the stock 303 as documented by Robin Whittle (Devil Fish) and published analyses of its diode-ladder filter.

  • Saw / Square: the square is made from the saw by a transistor shaper, as on the original, so it is not a perfect square.
  • Cutoff, Resonance, Env mod, Decay (200 ms–2 s filter envelope) and Accent work like the 303's knobs. The filter does not follow the keyboard and rings hard at high resonance but never self-oscillates, like a stock unit.
  • Accent: notes played hard (top of the pad, or high velocity from the MPC or a keyboard), or any note while the Accent latch is on, are accented: louder, brighter, with the envelope forced to 200 ms. With resonance up, a run of accents builds up the squeal, because the accent circuit's capacitor keeps some charge between notes.
  • Slide: a note that starts while the previous one is still held glides into it over the 303's 60 ms without restarting the envelopes. The Slide latch makes every note you play or record slide.
  • Notes keep their accent and slide when recorded with Overdub, so patterns play back with the same squelch.
  • For a classic acid line: Saw, Cutoff around 400–600 Hz, Resonance 7–9, Env mod 5–7, Decay low, Accent high, then add the Mixer's Saturator.
Moog Taurus

Any synth track can be a Moog Taurus bass pedal: on the Synth tab, change Synth to Moog Taurus. It is modeled from the Taurus I (Model 205A) owner's & service manual and schematic. It is monophonic and plays the lowest note held, like the pedals.

  • Presets: Taurus (both oscillators in unison, slow swell, big filter sweep and long decay), Tuba (second oscillator an octave up, gentle filter), Bass (punchy, steady filter) and Variable, which opens the Variables box. The fixed presets use the original resistor values for their envelopes, filter offset and oscillator mix.
  • Foot switches: Octave moves from 16' up to 8'; Glide slides between notes (time set by the Glide slider, up to 0.5 s); Decay lets notes ring out after release, otherwise they stop quickly.
  • Foot filter opens the filter up to 3 octaves and Foot volume sets the level, like the two foot sliders. Beat detunes the second oscillator up to 2% for the classic chorusing; Tune covers ±8%.
  • Variables box (Variable preset): octave Lo/Med/Hi, Osc B frequency (198–484 Hz), B-mix-A, Cut-off (7 octaves from 40 Hz), Emphasis (self-oscillates from about 8), Contour amount (up to 7 octaves), filter attack 5–56 ms and decay 50 ms–2.8 s, loudness attack 5–560 ms, decay 50 ms–2.8 s and sustain.

Tip: the Taurus preset with Decay on and the foot filter around half way is the classic huge pedal tone. Play it from the low octave of the grid or route your MPC or a keyboard to it.

Vocoder (SVC-350 model)

The vocoder makes a synth “talk”: your voice shapes the synth's sound. It is modeled on the Roland SVC-350's circuit from its 1979 service notes.

  1. On the Synth tab, turn Vocoder on. That synth becomes the instrument.
  2. Voice: Live input uses your USB-C mic; or pick an audio track with a recorded vocal (mute that track in the mixer if you only want the vocoder).
  3. Hold chords on the grid (or your keyboard/MPC) and talk or sing.
  • 11 sliders: the original frequency section, 10 bands (about 160 Hz to 5 kHz) plus HF, the high consonant filter that lets “s” and “t” through.
  • Balance: from vocoder only to dry voice.
  • Harmonics: brightness of the instrument going in. Brighter, buzzier synths (saw waves) are more understandable.
  • Ensemble: the original stereo chorus.
  • Hold: press and hold to freeze the last vowel, like the footswitch.

The vocoder is silent when no instrument is playing. The original's sound detector does the same, so breathing and room noise don't leak through.

Carrier, growl and presets

  • Carrier: the vocoder normally plays from this track's own synth. Pick another synth track to use its sound instead (for example a TB-303 or Taurus line), and mute that track in the mixer if you only want the robot.
  • Growl: the buzzy, gritty low end of early-80s electro vocals. It adds a square wave one octave below the carrier (a flip-flop divider, like an octave pedal) and overdrives the carrier before the filter bank, keeping the same loudness.
  • Presets: SVC-350 clean, Electro growl, Space robot, Egypt robot, Jet robot (bright), Metal monster, and Change the Beat ('fresh'). That last one sets up a deep, slow talking-machine line, "Ahhhh, this stuff is really fresh", feeds it to the vocoder and holds a low note under it.

Speech (talking machine)

  • Type lines in the Speech box, one per line. Preview plays the raw voice. Send to vocoder builds a muted Speech audio track from the lines and sets it as this vocoder's voice. If the carrier synth has no notes, a held low note is added so it talks straight away; play or record your own notes to change the pitch.
  • Voice: Robot (monotone), Deep, Normal or High. Pitch and Speed fine-tune it. Timing: each line starts on its own bar, or lines run back to back.
  • It's a formant synthesizer with English pronunciation rules, like the early-80s talking chips. Spell a word your own way if it comes out wrong, or type phonemes between slashes: /F R EH SH/. Repeating a vowel stretches it ("Ahhhh", "freeesh").
Mixer and effects
  • Each strip has pan, a fader with meter (double-tap to reset), Mute and Solo. FX opens the track's effects below.
  • Effects run in this order: Vocoder (synths) → Saturator → Redux → EQ Three → Auto Filter → Chorus → Compressor → Sidechain duck → Pan/Auto Pan → Fader → Sends.
  • EQ Three: low, mid and high, ±15 dB. Auto Filter: low-, high- or band-pass with cutoff and resonance.
  • Saturator adds warm drive. Redux crushes bits and sample rate. 12-bit 26k sets Redux to the SP-1200's exact rate and depth.
  • Chorus widens. Auto Pan moves the sound left and right at a tempo-synced rate.
  • Sidechain duck: the track dips each time the chosen pad hits (usually the kick). Release sets how fast it comes back.
  • Compressor evens out the level. At 0% it is fully bypassed.
  • Sends go to the shared Reverb and Delay. Set their size, time and feedback on the Master strip.
  • Sends to the FX rack feed the five rack units below the mixer (see FX rack).
  • Master EQ (Master strip → FX): low cut, plus ±12 dB at 80 Hz, 200 Hz, 500 Hz, 1.2 kHz, 3 kHz, 6 kHz and 12 kHz. It's switched out when flat.
  • EQ match: tap Load reference and pick a song whose tone you like, then Match my mix. The app renders your song, compares the two average spectra in 31 bands (1/3 octave) and sets a matching EQ, up to ±9 dB. Amount sets how much of it to use; the graph shows the reference (blue), your mix (white) and the correction (orange). Match again after big changes.
  • Master: Glue compression (adds 6 ms of latency while on, so leave it off while recording live) and a soft clipper that stops harsh overs.
FX rack

Below the mixer is a rack of five classic studio units. Each one is a shared send effect: turn up a track's Sends to the FX rack slider (Mixer → FX on that strip) to feed it. Every unit has a Presets menu, its own menus, and knobs. A unit nothing is sent to uses no CPU.

  • Lexicon PrimeTime (Model 93): two delays, A on the left and B on the right, each up to 128 ms × Multiply. As on the original, a bigger multiplier gives longer delays but less bandwidth (×1 12 kHz, ×2 6 kHz, ×4 3 kHz, ×8 1.5 kHz). A time/B time can follow the tempo (dotted 1/8 for that "Sucker MC's" exit), and Multiply rises automatically if a synced time needs it. Recirc feeds back through the High cut filter, normal or inverted. The VCO sweeps the delay over 2:1 for flanging and chorus.
  • Deltalab Effectron (ADM 1024): a 1-bit adaptive delta-modulation delay. Pick a Range (flange, double, short or long echo). The longer the delay inside a range, the slower the coder runs and the grittier it gets. ADM grit adds more. Pan puts the copy in the centre, to one side (Haas: about 22 ms on the right makes a vocal wide without an echo), or as the wide stereo-simulation pair. Regen can be negative (inverted phase).
  • Eventide H910 Harmonizer: one side is pitched up and the other down by Detune cents, each with its own delay up to 112.5 ms. ±9 cents at 15/25 ms is the classic doubled-vocal wall. Raw splicing keeps the H910's audible splice clicks and compander pumping; Smooth is the later de-glitched sound. Feedback goes past 100% like the original.
  • MXR 113: a 10-bit digital delay inside a compander. Bandwidth trades tone for time (20 kHz up to 160 ms … 2.5 kHz up to 1.28 s), so long repeats get darker and grainier. Regen at 100% holds the repeats.
  • Lexicon 224 / AMS RMX16: 224 programs (Vocal Plate, Percussion Plate, Concert Hall, Room, Chamber) run at the 224's narrow ~8 kHz bandwidth with its 12-bit grain. The RMX16 programs are full-bandwidth: Plate A1, Hall C1, Ambience, Nonlin 2 (the gated snare: holds, then stops dead) and Reverse 1 (builds up, then stops). Size sets the decay time or gate length, and Treble sets how fast the highs die.
Automation
  1. On the Mixer tab, turn on Automate (it turns red).
  2. Press play and move any fader, pan, EQ, filter cutoff/resonance or send. The moves are recorded into the loop.
  3. Turn Automate off. The moves play back every pass.
  • Touching an automated control while not recording takes it over by hand. Re-enable automation hands it back.
  • Each track's FX panel lists its automation with a small graph and Clear.
  • Automation belongs to the scene, so each scene can have its own sweeps. It is included in exports.
Scenes and Song mode
  • A scene holds its own drum hits, synth notes, audio clips, automation and loop length. Pads, samples, synth sounds and the mixer are shared.
  • Scene buttons sit above the drum pads. + copies the current scene so you can make a variation.
  • Tap a scene while playing and it starts at the next bar, or at the end of the loop if you set Launch that way (Song tab).
  • On the Song tab you can rename scenes, change their length and delete them.
  • Song: add parts (scene × repeats), reorder with ↑, then turn Song on and press play. It plays the parts in order and stops at the end. Export renders the whole song.
MIDI and syncing with your MPC
  1. Connect the MPC to the phone with a USB-C cable, or through a USB MIDI interface.
  2. Setup → MIDI sync → Connect MIDI, and allow it when Chrome asks. The app picks the MPC under From and To; choose it if not.
  • Follow MPC: press play on the MPC and this app starts with it, at its tempo, and stops with it. On the MPC, turn on sending MIDI clock (and start/stop) in its MIDI or sync settings.
  • Lead MPC: this app sends tempo, start and stop. On the MPC, turn on receiving MIDI clock.
  • Offset: if the two sound out of step, move it until the kicks line up. Positive delays this app.
  • Play these pads from the MPC's pads: MPC pads trigger the app's pads, with velocity, and are recorded with Overdub. Note repeat and erase work too.
  • A01 note / Learn: select A01 on the Drums tab, tap Learn, then hit the MPC pad that should play it. The rest follow in order.
  • Notes play: send incoming notes to the drum pads or to a synth (for a MIDI keyboard). Channel filters which MIDI channel is listened to.

MIDI learn (map MPC knobs and pads to any control):

  1. Setup → MIDI sync → MIDI learn. An orange bar appears, and every control you can map gets a dashed outline.
  2. Go to any tab and tap a slider, menu, number box or button. It gets a white outline.
  3. Move a knob on the MPC (or hit a pad). That control now follows it, and turns green.
  4. Map as many as you like, then tap Done.
  • Knobs move sliders across their full range, step through menus, and press buttons when turned past halfway. Pads press buttons (Play, Overdub, Mute…), step a menu to its next choice, or flip a slider between its lowest and highest setting.
  • Mapped controls work from any tab, even when that page isn't open. Moving a mapped track control while Automate is on records automation.
  • A note you map to a control no longer plays a pad. Mapping the same knob again moves it to the new control.
  • Your mappings are listed under MIDI learn. Remove one with ✕, or use Clear all mappings. They are saved with the project.
Exporting: WAV, MP3, stems and MIDI
  • Full mix: everything you hear, as one file.
  • Every track (ZIP): the mix plus each track rendered on its own through its effects and sends (muted tracks are skipped). All files are the same length so they line up from bar 1 in any DAW or on the MPC.
  • MIDI file: drum hits on channel 10 (A01 = the A01 note set in MIDI settings, C1 by default) and each synth on its own channel, at the project tempo with swing applied.
  • Format: WAV 16-bit, WAV 24-bit (best for further mixing), MP3 320 or 192 kbps. Rate: 44.1 or 48 kHz.
  • Repeat: how many times to loop the current scene. With Song mode on, the whole song is exported instead.
  • Files go to your Downloads folder. Share sends the last export straight to another app (Drive, WhatsApp, a DAW…).
Look

The app is styled after the Akai MPC60 (warm-grey case, blue backlit display with white lettering, dark rubber pads, mode keys with red LEDs) and the E-mu SP-1200 (charcoal panel, white silkscreen, slider faders). Settings work like the MPC's: − and + step a value (hold to scroll), and the value itself is a drop-down to jump straight to a setting.

Saving, updates and storage
  • The project, samples and recordings save automatically in this browser on this phone. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Clearing Chrome's site data for this app, or uninstalling Chrome, deletes the project. Export anything you want to keep.
  • New project (Setup, tap twice) erases everything and starts fresh.
  • Updates arrive automatically. If something looks old, close the app fully and open it again.
Troubleshooting
No sound
Tap any pad once: phones only start audio after a touch. Check the phone isn't on silent and the Master fader is up. If a track is soloed (S lit), only soloed tracks play.
The interface or mic isn't found
Unplug and replug it, then tap Enable input again. Check Chrome has microphone permission (lock icon in the address bar → Permissions). Some interfaces need to be plugged in before the app opens.
Recordings or pads are out of time (latency)
For audio takes, adjust Setup → Recording → Offset. For pad hits, raise Pad timing. Turn off master Glue while recording to save 6 ms.
MIDI device doesn't show up
Tap Refresh MIDI devices. Make sure the MPC's USB mode allows MIDI and the cable carries data (some charge-only cables don't).
The MPC and app drift apart
Only one device should send clock. Use Follow MPC or Lead MPC, not both, and make sure the MPC isn't also receiving clock from somewhere else.
Crackles or dropouts
Go to Setup → Performance → Audio buffer and choose Medium or Large. This is the biggest fix. Also close other apps and turn off battery saver. The Prophet-5 and Solina use the most power when many notes are held, and very long Complex-warped clips at odd tempos add load too. An effect set to zero is switched off completely, so it costs nothing.
MP3 export fails
The MP3 encoder downloads the first time you use it. Connect to the internet once, or export WAV.
“Could not read that file”
Use WAV, AIFF, MP3, FLAC or OGG. Some phone voice-recorder formats aren't supported.
Workflow tips
  • Build a beat in Scene 1, tap + to copy it, then strip it down for a verse or add hats for the hook.
  • Record with Timing Off for feel, then Apply 1/16 to individual takes if they need tightening.
  • Chop a stem: Stems → Chop → From track → Transients → Send to pads with Make a pattern on, then replay it your own way.
  • Duck pads and bass from the kick (Sidechain duck, 40–60%) for a pumping mix.
  • Export Every track as WAV 24-bit to finish a song in another DAW or load parts into your MPC.
