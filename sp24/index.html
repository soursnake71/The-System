The SP's MIDI clock runs at 24 PPQ, the same resolution as its sequencer, so each clock moves the playhead one tick. Start, Continue, Stop and Song Position are followed. Set the SP to send MIDI clock (SP-1200: Sync = MIDI out; SP-12: MIDI setup).

How the SP sequencer is modelled

    
In SP terms a pattern is a segment. A segment holds the notes recorded into it; a song chains segments. Each note is an event: its position at 24 PPQ (24 ticks per quarter note, 96 in a 4/4 bar), the sound it plays (one of 32: bank 1–4, pad 1–8), its level (0–31) and its tune (−8 to +7). On a sound in Decay mode the tune value is a decay offset instead.

    
Swing on the SP is a setting of the whole segment: every note exactly on the second 8th of a beat plays later by the same amount (54, 58, 63, 67 or 71 % = 13–17 ticks instead of 12), a chord moves as one, and the swing restarts at every bar line. The roll draws those notes where they play (a faint outline marks the stored position) and the preview plays them there.

    
Selective swing moves only the selected notes, at the SP's 8th amounts or as 16th swing (58, 67 or 75 % = 7, 8 or 9 ticks instead of 6) without the double-tempo trick.

    
Preview audio is an approximation: loaded samples are pitched with the SP-1200 semitone table (from libmd12, MIT), other pads use simple synth voices.
